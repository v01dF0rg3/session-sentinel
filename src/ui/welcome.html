What it will do

  • When you close the browser, sessions on your high-risk sites are cleared. If Chrome exits before that finishes, it completes on next launch, before your tabs reload.
  • After 30 minutes idle, or when your screen locks, the same thing happens.
  • Where a site offers a real sign out of all devices control, it is used, so the session dies on your other devices too. Where it does not, only local data is cleared — and it says so rather than claiming more.

Lower-risk sites are left alone by default. You can change any of this in settings.

    Two honest limits

    Deleting cookies protects this computer. It does not invalidate a token that was already stolen and is being used elsewhere. Only the sites with a real revoke control can do that, and results say which is which.

    You can be signed out at an awkward moment. That is the feature working. Use Never clear this site on anything where that would hurt.

    Clearing a site does not always end its session. Where the site offers a sign-out, this uses it and the session really ends. Where it does not, deleting the cookies only abandons the session: it stays live on the site, listed as active, just no longer visible to you. Results say which happened, and if you ever think an account has been taken, the Been hacked? link walks you through securing everything in the order that makes it stick.
