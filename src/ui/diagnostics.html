Diagnostics

Exercises the browser APIs Session Sentinel depends on and reports what actually works here. Safe to run: it reads your cookie list but does not delete any of it, and the only data it clears belongs to a reserved test domain that cannot exist.

Why this page exists. The logic that decides what to delete is covered by automated tests, but the browser APIs that do the deleting can only be exercised inside a real installed extension. This is how we find out whether they behave on your Chrome before you trust the extension with a real account.

CheckResult

Coverage

How often the extension reaches a site's real sign-out, rather than only deleting cookies and leaving the session running. Four sites have a hand-written recipe; every other site relies on a generic fallback that has never been measured. This counts results as you use it, so the sites worth a recipe are chosen by evidence.

Why these sites are listed

A site is called signed in when it has one convincing auth cookie, or two near-misses. Plenty of sites set server-side cookies for visitors who have never had an account — eBay's nonsession is one — so if something here is not an account of yours, the cookie that caused it is named below. Send it over and the rule gets fixed. Names only; values are never shown or copied.

Activity log

Every step of every run, kept across browser restarts. If Chrome closes during a logout, this shows exactly where it got to — look for whether run:complete appears before the next browser:startup.
