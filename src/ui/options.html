Session Sentinel

Everything below is stored on this computer. The extension has no account, sends no telemetry, and never transmits your browsing history anywhere.

What sign-out can and cannot prove. Session Sentinel can activate a site's logout and clear data on this computer, but a click does not prove the server rejected a copied token. It reports sign-out attempted unless a separately tested revoke-everywhere recipe completed. Local clearing never invalidates a token that was already stolen.

Automatic triggers

When the browser closes

Chrome gives extensions no reliable shutdown hook. Cleanup is attempted at close; if the prior run did not finish, it is retried after the next launch and matching restored tabs are reloaded. This is not an exact shutdown guarantee.

After inactivity

Applies to sites at or above the selected risk level.

min

When the screen locks or sleeps

A deliberate "I am walking away" signal, so it is treated more aggressively.

How hard to try

Attempt site sign-out

Uses a temporary background tab in an existing window to activate the site's own sign-out before local cleanup. Only the site can invalidate its token, and the extension does not treat a click as proof. Adds a few seconds per site.

Per-site data removal

Deep also clears IndexedDB and cache storage. Some sites keep drafts and offline documents there, so deep is reserved for the tiers where that is unlikely.

Order accounts by how often you use them

When two confirmed accounts are equally sensitive, put the one you actually live in first in the recovery list. A visit never turns a site into an account — sites Chrome cannot settle stay in the separate review queue. This never changes how risky a site is considered: a news site read daily is not more dangerous to lose than a bank visited twice a year. Needs permission to read your most-visited sites, which Chrome will ask for. Nothing about your browsing leaves this machine.

Warn before sign-out when an account may be compromised

The warning starts with a clean-device recovery checklist. It tells you to review active sessions or devices, remove unfamiliar access, change an exposed password, and review MFA and recovery methods. It never assumes a password change or a clicked logout invalidated every token.

Your open tabs

A page you already have open keeps its session in memory, so it carries on looking signed in until it is reloaded. Reloading it makes the local cleanup visible immediately. Turn this off if you would rather refresh pages yourself; note that reloading discards anything you had typed into a form on that site.

Show a notification after each run

Reports sign-out attempts, local cleanup, and results that need attention. Remote token revocation is not verified.

Sign-out recipes

Keep site recipes up to date

Sites move their "sign out everywhere" buttons, and a stale recipe must never keep a revocation claim alive. Updates are signed, and the whole list is downloaded at once — never queried per site — so the server cannot learn which sites you use. If an update fails or cannot be verified, the recipes built into the extension stay in force.

Sites

Set a site to never clear to keep its local data — it is skipped by every automatic trigger and by "Attempt sign-out of confirmed accounts". You can still clear it by hand from the popup. Always protect does the opposite: the site is treated as critical no matter how it was classified.
